=====================================================================
 09 - MVCC: MANY SESSIONS WITHOUT FIGHTING
=====================================================================

THE ANALOGY: READING A STABLE COPY

You are reading page 40 of a shared document. Meanwhile a colleague
edits page 90 and saves. Your reading is not disturbed - you were
reading a snapshot from before their save. When you finish and open
the document again, you see the new version.

MVCC (Multi-Version Concurrency Control) gives every database session
such snapshots:

    READ COMMITTED  - each statement sees everything committed before
                      that statement started.
    REPEATABLE READ - inside BEGIN...COMMIT, your session keeps its
                      old snapshot even if others commit new data.

HOW LEAFDB DOES IT

Sessions don't share memory. Each Database object has its own buffer
pool. Commits append atomic batches to the WAL (chapter 05). Between
statements, a session peeks at the WAL tail: "any new committed
batches? then adopt them". Inside an explicit transaction it stops
peeking - the snapshot stays frozen.

Because commits are single all-or-nothing batches, a reader can never
see half of someone else's transaction. This is enforced by a stress
test where one session writes rows in groups of 10 while another
counts them forever - counts must always be multiples of 10.

READERS DON'T BLOCK THE WRITER

A reader holding an old snapshot never asks the writer to wait, and
the writer's commits never disturb readers. The test suite runs a
reader mid-scan while 400 commits happen; both sides finish happily.

THE WRITER LOCK

Only actual WRITE statements take the shared per-database lock, and
only for the duration of that write. Two sessions trying to modify
data at once queue up politely; readers walk straight past.

CHECKPOINTING WITH COMPANY

The WAL can only be reset (checkpointed) when no other session might
still need its older contents. LeafDB tracks live sessions and
refuses checkpointing while they exist; the last session to close
checkpoints automatically.
