=====================================================================
 03 - THE B+ TREE (THE HEART OF EVERYTHING)
=====================================================================

THE PROBLEM

Your table has 1,000,000 rows stored across ~4,000 pages. You want
the row with id = 424242. Scanning every page is too slow. What you
need is a structure that stays SORTED and lets you jump straight to
the right page.

THE ANALOGY: A LIBRARY

Imagine books arranged in order by their number. At the entrance
there's a sign: "numbers 1-1000 -> aisle 1, 1001-2000 -> aisle 2".
Inside each aisle another sign narrows it down to a shelf. You never
walk past more than a few signs - you go straight to your shelf.

A B+ TREE is exactly this:

    - at the bottom: LEAF pages holding the actual rows, sorted
    - above them: INTERNAL pages acting as signs ("keys >= 500 ->
      go right")
    - one top page: the ROOT

FINDING id = 424242 ("a search")

Start at the root. Each internal page says which child to enter.
Descend 2-3 levels. Arrive at the leaf containing your key. Binary-
search inside it. Done - in 3 hops instead of reading 4,000 pages.

That is why lookups are O(log n): even with a million rows, the tree
is only 3-4 levels deep, because each level points to hundreds of
children.

RANGE SCANS: THE SUPERPOWER

Leaf pages are also LINKED left-to-right like beads on a string.
"Give me ids 100 through 200" becomes: hop to the leaf where 100
lives, then walk right along the chain until you pass 200. A hash
table (another famous index) can find single keys but cannot do this
- that is why databases use trees for the main index.

INSERTING: SPLITS

Insert into the sorted leaf. If the leaf overflows its 4 KB, split it
into two halves and give the parent a separator key pointing to the
new right half. If the parent overflows too, split upward. If the
root splits, a NEW root is created - that is how the tree grows
downward while every leaf stays at the same depth. Balance for free.
