=====================================================================
 04 - DELETING AND REBALANCING (KEEPING THE TREE HEALTHY)
=====================================================================

THE PROBLEM WITH DELETING

Deleting is harder than inserting. If you just remove a key from a
leaf, that leaf may end up nearly empty. A tree full of half-empty
pages wastes space and makes scans read more pages than needed.

THE ANALOGY: TWO FRIDGE SHELVES

Two shelves each hold up to 10 eggs. You want them roughly balanced.
If one shelf drops to 2 eggs while the neighbor has 9, you don't
rebuild the fridge - you BORROW an egg from the neighbor. Only if a
shelf becomes completely empty do you MERGE it with its neighbor and
remove the divider sign above.

LeafDB does exactly this:

  1. delete the row from its leaf
  2. if the leaf is now too empty:
       - try to BORROW one row from the left or right sibling
         (and fix the separator in the parent)
  3. if no sibling can spare, MERGE this leaf with a sibling
     and remove their separator from the parent
  4. if the parent itself gets too empty, repeat upward
  5. if the root ends up with a single child, that child becomes
     the new root and the tree shrinks by one level

A HONEST SHORTCUT: "SEPARATORS MAY LAG"

When the smallest key of a leaf is deleted, real databases sometimes
leave the old separator above it untouched. LeafDB allows this too.
The rule it enforces is only: separator must never be BIGGER than the
smallest key actually in the right subtree. Search still works, we
just avoid extra writes. This trick is written down in check() so
tests enforce it forever.

check(): THE TREE DOCTOR

btree.py has a function called check() that walks the whole tree and
verifies: sorted keys everywhere, all leaves at equal depth, chain of
leaves matches tree order, no node too empty. The random fuzz tests
call it every few hundred operations - it has caught real bugs (see
DEVLOG.md, "merges need the separator").
