=====================================================================
 06 - TRANSACTIONS (BEGIN, COMMIT, ROLLBACK)
=====================================================================

THE ANALOGY: DRAFTS ON SCRATCH PAPER

You are rearranging three paragraphs of an essay. You don't edit the
final document directly - you work on scratch paper. If it looks
good, copy it in (COMMIT). If it's a mess, throw the paper away and
the essay is untouched (ROLLBACK).

A transaction is that scratch paper for database changes:

    BEGIN;
    DELETE FROM orders WHERE user_id = 7;   -- not visible yet!
    INSERT INTO archive ...                  -- not visible yet!
    COMMIT;                                  -- now all at once

Two guarantees matter:

  ATOMICITY - all changes land together or none do. There is no such
  thing as "half a transaction".

  ISOLATION - other sessions cannot see your draft.

HOW LEAFDB DOES IT: SHADOW PAGES

While a transaction runs, every page it touches keeps its OLD image
in memory (a "shadow"). The live tree uses the new images. If you
ROLLBACK, LeafDB throws away the new page copies and reinstates the
shadows - done. Because dirty pages are never allowed to leave the
memory cache during a transaction (eviction pauses), uncommitted
data provably never reaches the data file. Nothing to undo on disk.

If you COMMIT instead, the changed pages are appended to the WAL with
a commit record (previous chapter) and become permanent.

AUTOCOMMIT

Every single statement outside BEGIN/COMMIT is wrapped in its own
tiny transaction automatically. That is why a lone INSERT still
survives crashes.

MULTI-STATEMENT TRANSACTIONS

BEGIN ... COMMIT may span many statements and minutes. A bank
transfer ("take 10 from A, add 10 to B") must be one transaction -
if the power dies between the two updates, rollback guarantees the
money didn't vanish into air.
